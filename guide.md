
# Isle 2 — How to Play (updated)

Welcome to Isle 2. Shared economy across world map, two minigames, shop, NPCs and upgrades.

## Quick reference
- The loop is **Reef → Upcycle → Shop**, north→south→east, marked by the flow dots.
- Every day has a **Tide**: Glass Calm / Eel Run / Slack Water / Night Drift.
  The almanac logs each you witness (`almanac n/4`).
- The dock sells a **dockhand's tip** ($25, $15 on a 3-day streak) for
  tomorrow's tide.
- **Tide Ledger** on the stats page holds the seven notes — read them all.
- Watch the island stretch you thin, then come back tomorrow. The tide never
  blinks.

## Controls
- The main menu carries the island's one-line motto under the buttons:
  "Keep the shore" — the keeper's whole job, one sentence.

- The main menu's continue panel and the HUD both carry today's tide
  ("≈ <tide> · day N") — the sea greets you before you press Continue.
- **Hide the tide** — settings toggle for keepers who trust their own eyes.
  Every surface goes quiet ("The sea keeps its own counsel") instead of
  reading the day's tide — the rules, the rotation and the almanac all
  stay exactly the same. For people who want the sea in front of them,
  not behind them.
  The hidden horizon stays legal: THE QUIET KEEPER rewards three such
  runs, and THE TIDE WALKER belongs to those who tried both ways.


- **World map** — tap anywhere to walk there; the trail and flow dots point
  at the recommended next stop on the circuit.
- **Upcycle bench** — swipe to move between bins; tap the carried item to
  inspect what it is; drop it in the matching bin. Swipe threshold is 30 px.
- **Reef Rescue** — drag to steer the drone, momentum matters. The drone
  auto-sucks in debris and fish bumps end the dive (three bumps by default);
  ESC (or the on-screen Return to Map) ends the dive safe.
- **Shop** — Sell 1 / Sell 5 / Sell All per material; Undo rewinds a whole
  sale atomically, all-or-nothing.
- **Everywhere** — ESC (or Tab on the island menu) closes the current card
  (modal first, then leaving the scene). The island menu opens with ☰.

## Economy basics
- Materials: Polyethylene(1kg), Polypropylene(2kg), Glass(3kg), Nylon(2kg), Reef Debris(1kg).
- Bag capacity starts at 10kg and grows by +10kg per level.

### Pacing (measured on a fresh save, three consecutive loops)
- A surface dive fishes ~16% of spawns as fish (~27% at 200 m, ~43% at 400 m,
  ~57% at 600 m). Each garbage pickup banks 1 kg toward the end-of-dive payout.
- Loop yields: +$34, +$32, +$32 (≈$17 of materials + the flat $15 island-circuit
  bonus; bulk ×1.25 hits whenever a single material reaches 5+).
- Market Contact (80$) lands after ~2–3 loops (~10–12 minutes); Bag and Reef
  Charter sinks are a 3–5 loop goal. Pacing is intentional — the island circuit
  is the reliable earner, bulk sales reward focused hauls over mixed ones.
- A typical 60–90 s dive banks ~15–30 kg of Reef Debris, which the upcycle
  converts one-for-one into materials (each correct sort burns 1 debris).
- The opening loop (dive → sort → sell) pays out in ~3–6 minutes.
- Bag capacity starts at 10kg and grows by +10kg per level. Two ways to upgrade:
  trash-paid at the world menu (6 + 3*level), or money-paid at the shop (60 + 25*level).
- Shop sells at rates: Polyethylene 2, Polypropylene 3, Glass 5, Nylon 4, Reef Debris 1.
- Bulk bonus: selling 5+ of a type gives +25% — and +35% on **Glass Calm** days (the harbor pays over the odds for a full load while the reef emptied out overnight).
- Sell controls: Sell 1, Sell 5, Sell All. Undo last sale is all-or-nothing — if the
  bag cannot fit the whole lot back in, the undo is refused (tooltip tells you how
  many kg you need free) rather than returning partial goods for the full price.
  Undo plays a short rewind animation (REVERSED stamp, exiting receipt).

## Shop conveniences (money sinks)
- Bag +10 kg — 60 + 25 per level. The only way to buy space with money.
- Market Contact — 80/140/200. Each level adds +1$ to every unit sold, on top of
  the bulk bonus, so it pays back across all material types.
- Reef Charter — 100/180. Each level tolerates one extra fish bump per dive
  (the base allowance is 3).
- Money comes from sales and from the $15 island-circuit bonus, so the shop has
  somewhere to spend it beyond bag space.
- Patron of the Isle — 1500$ one time. Pure prestige title shown on the stats
  page, plus the PATRON OF THE ISLE achievement.
- Tidekeeper's Pact — 2500$ one time. The final sink: a permanent +10 kg bag
  and the KEEPER OF THE TIDE achievement (no prestige-only fill — the reward is
  mechanical). Funding it AND reading all three shore bottles washes a fourth,
  hidden reply bottle up on the south beach — the tide answers its keeper.
  Funded pacts also earn the island's harbor gossip: NPCs occasionally trade a
  mint-green nod among their idle lines.

## Money-sink payback audit (re-derived from measured $32/loop)
*Measured on a fresh save, three consecutive full loops ≈ $32 each; a loop is
dives → sortie → sell, ~$17 materials plus the $15 circuit bonus. All prices
checked against this yield — nothing changed, deliberately.*
- **Bag +10 kg (60+25$/level):** at a 10 kg hold, bag space is the binding
  constraint. Level 1 roughly doubles cargo per run (≈$17→~$30 materials), so
  it repays in **~3 loops** and every loop afterwards. The single best first
  purchase — this is what the **Room for more?** nudge points at.
- **Trash-priced bag (6+3 trash/level, island terminal):** the low-cash route.
  One dive banks ~15–30 kg Reef Debris; converting that to capacity instead of
  materials is the right deal when money is tight.
- **Market Contact 80/140/200$:** each level is +$1 per unit. A 10-unit haul
  earns +$10/level, so **Level 1 pays back in ~8 loops**; Levels 2–3 extend the
  same economics over the mid-game. It is deliberately the slowest normal sink
  per dollar — the $200 third level is the long-game stretch. Left as priced.
- **Reef Charter 100/180$:** pure survival (one extra fish bump per level) —
  it buys depth access rather than money, so it is priced by feel, not payback.
- **Circuit bonus $15:** the flat, guaranteed component of loop income (~47%);
  it is the stabiliser that stops the whole economy from depending on RNG
  hauls. Not reduced.
- **Patron of the Isle 1500$:** aspirational finish line, ~47 median loops —
  the game's final money sink.
- **Dockhand's tide tip 25$:** not an investment but information — one day of
  tomorrow's weather, paid once, gone at midnight. Cheap enough to buy every
  few days (~1 loop's income), priced to stay tempting rather than strategic.

## First playthrough (interactive tutorial)
- On a fresh save, world map load shows the "Welcome to Isle 2" card (once per save).
- The TutorialDirector then arms a five-step tour, one hint card at a time:
  1. **Take a stroll** — tap to walk; completes on your first step.
  2. **Dive the reef** — head to the south dock; completes when the dive begins.
  3. **Sort at the bench** — ride the belt east; completes on your first correct sort.
  4. **Cash in at the shop** — north; completes on your first sale (5+ pays +25% bulk).
  5. **You know the loop!** — celebratory card naming the $15 island-circuit bonus.
- Cards are non-modal (they never pause or block play) and only appear in the scene
  where the current step can be acted on; they reappear on scene re-entry until done.
- Progress saves as `tutorial_step`; the tour survives quitting mid-way and never
  re-arms on old saves.
- Eight one-shot ambient nudges also live here: a **Bag almost full!** card the
  first time your hold crosses 85% (fires once per save, skipped while another
  card is up), a **Bottles on the shore** card shown right after the tour
  finishes, a **Room for more?** card after your first sale — pointing at
  both bag-expansion paths (shop cash or island-map trash), an **Oops — bulk
  sale?** card after your first bulk sale — pointing at the Undo last sale
  button — a **Tune up the dock** card the first time you can afford a
  speed/range tier, naming the live trash cost and reminding you upgrades
  spend materials, not money, an **Islanders about** card on the first world
  entry after the tour while any resident is still unmet — pointing at the
  trail walkers and their first-visit gifts, a **Someone needs a hand** card
  the first time you hold enough material to turn in a quest — pointing at
  the quest tracker and the ★ glow, and a **Deep Cache** card the first time
  you can afford the 30-score unlock, pointing at the score-priced Deep Cache at
  the island menu (both a dive and a bench run feed the score). Nudges fire
  one card at a time: if a card or modal owns the screen, the nudge waits
  for it to close instead of evaporating.

## Daily tide (why today feels different)
- The HUD line counts your days on the island (day N) — the keeper-'s
  almanac reads the calendar, the HUD reads your history.
- The island itself wears the day's mood as a wash of colour over the
  map — you feel which tide it is before you read the HUD.
- The island has a new tide every local calendar day, shown as a line on the
  world HUD (`≈ <tide>` — hover it for the full reading) and announced by a **TIDE REPORT** card the first time you
  enter the world each day. The tide is the same for every player on that day
  — it is a calendar rotation, not a random roll — and it resets with the
  island's save like everything else.
- Four conditions rotate:
  - **Glass Calm** — the reef lets go of its litter: noticeably more debris
    and fewer fish in Reef Rescue, and the shop's bulk bonus rises to +35%.
  - **Eel Run** — a warm current: reef fish dart about 28% faster. Lead your
    drag a little more.
  - **Slack Water** — the upcycle belt runs about 18% slower, which is the
    forgiving day to practise precise sorting.
  - **Night Drift** — golden debris and golden belt scrap are twice as likely,
    so it is the best day to chase GOLDEN TOUCH and a fat bench score.
- On Night Drift the shop log notes the moonlight on the till — Corl hums
  something with too many verses. No bonus; some nights pay in atmosphere.
- Day 100 on the island gets noted at the till — the island has a
  centennial, and Corl polishes the counter unprompted.
- The shop keeps a barometer above the spend rows — today's tide, one
  line, so the Glass Calm bulk premium is impossible to miss.
- The stats page's almanac line also carries your streak; once the book
  is filled it glows gold and the daily report says "almanac kept".
- The stats page's almanac line also carries your streak; once the book
  is filled it glows gold and the daily report says "almanac kept".
- With Hide the tide on, Corl sells "the quiet" instead — same tip, same
  tomorrow, no name ever spoken.
- The shop sells a **dockhand's tide tip** for $25 ($15 for keepers on a
  three-day streak — Corl respects regulars): it names tomorrow's
  tide for that one day, so a keeper who plans around the rotation can
  stock up, reposition or save the golden-scrap day for last — once bought,
  the world HUD's tide line grows a `Tomorrow:` line until midnight — and
  Oda will notice you paid the dockhand, if you ask her nicely — and the
  shop points it out first for any keeper holding 100$ or more.
- **Oda the tide-watcher** stands just west of Mara (she has no quests — she
  keeps the island's almanac). Every islander who notices the water repeats
  the day's observation, so dockside chatter always reflects the current tide.
- Come back after two or more days away and the island greets you once with
  a "Welcome back, keeper" card — the tide kept its four days without you.
- The first time the report card appears, the onboarding guide pipes up once
  ("The sea has moods") and explains the HUD line — returning keepers never
  see it again.
- The report card appears once per island per day; the HUD line stays up for
  the session, and its tooltip repeats the full explanation.
- Each new tide you witness is ticked into the **keeper's almanac** (the card
  says `almanac n/4`, the stats page lists all four with ✓/·). Log all four and
  THE FOUR TIDES achievement fires, followed by **THE ALMANAC PAGE** — the lost
  last entry of the keeper who never left the island — and a one-time +$30.
  Log all four and the keeper finally writes back: **THE KEEPER'S LETTER** is
  tucked on the west shore beside Oda, and reading it unlocks a collection badge (with THE WHISPERER, THE SILENT CHORUS, THE LONG KEEPER and THE YEAR KEEPER). The Tide Ledger on the stats page keeps a re-readable
  copy either way, and Oda will mention the first keeper finding his
  note — once you have it, she never stops mentioning him.
  The rotation is
  four days, so a full almanac takes four visits spread across at least four
  calendar days — every tide is guaranteed to come round.
- **Why is there a rotation at all?** Nobody agrees. Oda's version — the one
  written on the inside cover of her almanac — says the island was once a
  clock: a brass-hearted tide-keeper whom the sea adopted and the sea then
  disassembled, wheel by wheel, as a gift to time. Four wheels, four tides,
  four days. Mara rolls her eyes at it. Corl refuses to comment. Kai calls it
  "bad inventory timing." The keeper before you wrote his last entry the week
  the moon began showing its other side. Make of that what you will — and if
  the HUD line blinks twice in the same day, you have not taken it seriously
  enough.

## Upcycle
- The bench HUD shows today's tide inline — read the belt before you commit.
- Sort belt trash into matching bins. Wrong/burned does not produce materials or score loss reward except streak reset.
- Correct sort gains that material and updates recycled counter/belt counter HUD.
- Back on the map the run is titled like a chapter — "Bench Run № 2 —
  Hot Streak" — named by the longest streak you kept inside that run, and it
  flags a new personal best. Ten finished runs earns BENCH REGULAR.
- Mid-run the score readout carries "(best N)" so the chase stays visible, and
  passing the record fires a one-shot gold "NEW BEST — N!" callout with a punch.
- The pre-round briefing leads with your personal stake — "Best score: N ·
  Runs finished: M" — so the ghost follows you into the room.
- Combo milestones (every five clean sorts) add +5 score and a "FIVE ON THE
  BELT!" banner; a burned or wrong item resets the streak.
- When only 5 Reef Debris remain the bench warns you once; when the pile runs
  out it stops feeding the belt and lets you finish whatever is on it, then
  closes on a gold **SHIFT COMPLETE** panel (nothing left unsorted).
- The workshop hums while you sort (low drone + fan whir under the music); it is
  on the Ambience slider, not SFX.

## Reef Rescue
- Dodge fish, collide with garbage to collect 1kg per hit, combos multiply debris bonus.
- 3 fish hits ends run (Reef Charter at the shop raises this); escape early with ESC or button.
- Combo counter appears under the depth HUD from ×2 up and punches at every
  x3 milestone; depth milestones (100/250/400/700/900 m) fire a one-time banner
  each — 400 m is DEEP DIVE, 900 m is TO THE ABYSS.
- Off-screen edge markers: fish markers flare red and swell/throb as they
  close on the drone (proximity + depth-scaled shimmer); garbage markers stay calm yellow.
- Fish telegraph: a red wake blooms at the bottom edge in the fish's lane ~1.15s
  before it rises into view (swells and fades; skipped under reduced motion).
- One bump left from the fish limit: the screen edges breathe a soft red
  vignette (static dim under reduced motion).
- Dive summary payoff: when a dive beats your deepest ever, the summary leads
  with "★ NEW BEST DEPTH — N m! ★" and plays the achievement chime.
- Debris payout breakdown on the summary card — haul, deep-cache bonus and
  "bag full −N kg" so the capacity cap reads as a rule, not a bug.
- Rumour (Mara, if you ask nicely): someone watches the island from the
  far point. Nobody has caught them yet. Stand at the far-east peninsula and they will not run — they never do. Catching them once earns THE FAR POINT, the 38th badge — and the watcher sends a note east, waiting on the path you took to him. Reading it answers the whole arc: the island counts those the keeper keeps.
- The completion of the catalogue earns one last gift: THE FIFTH TIDE —
  a single legendary card, a low harbor bell and +$250. The island shows
  its keeper the one tide it never wrote in any almanac.
- Earn every badge on the island (ISLAND STEWARD) and the world itself
  wears a faint golden hush — a quiet victory the map never announces.
- The stats page's Tide Ledger and Reef Registry tease locked rows
  instead of printing question marks — each bottle and fish has its own
  spoiler-free hint; the page is the island's clue board.
- The stats page opens with a single-line Keeper's Journal ledger: "Journal — bottles n/8 · starfish n/5 · tides n/4 · species n/4 · achievements n/43" — so you can see the whole completion board at a glance without scrolling.
- Bottles you have already read can be re-read from the stats page via the Tide Ledger overlay (the game nudges you there once the third base bottle is read) (undiscovered notes stay locked).
- Quest economics audit (SALE_PRICES-weighted): turn-ins are deliberately slightly under market value — that is the price of quest progress and the Harbor Hero +10 kg cap — but the old numbers were punitive (Tom 24$->12$, Nina 30$->15$, Kai 25$->10$, Mara t2 25$->18$). Buffed: Tom t1 4->6 PP (18$), Nina t1 5->6 PP (18$), Kai t1 now 6 PP (18$), Mara t2 6->8 PP (24$). Nina t2 (+8$) and Kai t2 (+4$) remain the profitable tiers.
- Depth is tracked in the stats page and feeds two achievements: DEEP DIVE (400 m) and TO THE ABYSS (900 m). Ten finished dives earns REEF REGULAR; the stats page keeps a running "Field log" of dives and bench runs.
- Species you bump into are logged in the stats page ("Fish spotted") and in the Reef Registry overlay (tap a note to re-read what you know about that fish); meeting all four earns REEF REGISTRY.
- Five starfish hide around the island (the game nudges on your first and on your fourth) — walk over one to add it to the journal (stats page, "Starfish found"); collecting all five earns STARLIGHT; once you find your first starfish the world HUD keeps a running count (bottle-style line under the route tracker), flipping gold as STARLIGHT KEEPER at five; collecting every starfish also pays a one-time +$25 Tide Pool card.
- Once ISLAND STEWARD is earned, one more star appears at the far point east of the watcher: **THE KEEPER'S STAR** — the sixth star the island held back for the keeper who kept everything. Walking over it delivers the island's last card, a low bell, +$50 and the 43rd badge. It is not counted toward STARLIGHT, it never appears before the capstone, and it fires exactly once. The stats page's deeds column records it when found.
- A rare golden Goldie (10% of fish spawns, any species) pays +5 when bumped and costs no life — its edge marker shimmers gold, never red.
- Rare golden debris in the reef (12% spawn) is worth +1 bonus score on top of the combo multiplier.
- Rare golden belt items in Upcycle (12% spawn) are worth double score (+2) when sorted correctly.
- Closing your first full island circuit earns ROUND THE ISLE; 25 closes earns LOOP MASTER. Lifetime golden scraps (belt + reef + Goldies) persist and feed the GOLDEN TOUCH achievement at 10.
- A dive with no fish bumps and at least 8 kg hauled earns a CLEAN DIVE tag on the summary and recap cards.
- A belt run with no misses and at least 5 sorted items earns a FLAWLESS SHIFT tag on the summary and recap cards.
- Lifetime counters (stats page) track clean dives and flawless shifts; they feed the CLEAN SWEEP (5 clean dives) and SILK TOUCH (5 flawless shifts) achievements.
- Your longest sort streak persists as "Best sort streak" (stats page) and feeds the HOT STREAK achievement at 15 in a row.
- Your heaviest single dive persists as "Heaviest dive" (stats page) and feeds the HEAVY HARVEST achievement at 30 kg banked in one dive.
- Your longest single-dive garbage chain persists as "Best reef combo" (stats page) and feeds the RHYTHM OF THE REEF achievement at a 9-chain (a x4 score multiplier).
- Long-game badges: BENCH MASTER (25 runs), REEF VETERAN (25 dives) and
  LOOP MASTER (25 circuits) sit behind the 10-run regulars — the island's
  endgame chase alongside the two pact/patron money sinks.
- Garbage converts into Reef Debris (plus Deep Cache bonus per tier and capacity).
  The Deep Cache bonus is never lost to a full bag: any part that does not fit is
  salvaged as money ($1/kg, shown as "N$ salvaged" on the summary card). Without
  that, the upgrade would do nothing at the 10 kg starting bag, where every dive
  fills up.
- A dive that never reaches a pickup or a fish bump is not counted: no dive
  counter, no depth best, no "Dive № N" recap card, no haul ("No pickups this
  dive." on the summary card). Mirrors the bench's empty-shift rule. Ten finished
  *counted* dives still earns REEF REGULAR.
- A muffled underwater current runs under the dive music; trim it with the
  Ambience slider without quieting pickup or combo feedback.
- Closing a full island circuit (Reef → Upcycle → Shop) rings the harbor bell
  on the next map load.
- A distant foghorn sounds every 45–85 s while the sailboat is up on the
  horizon (it stays silent when the boat fades out; it still sounds under
  reduced motion, since it is audio, not motion).

## Saving & multiple islands
- Your island saves automatically (debounced) and on **Save & quit**. Each save
  also keeps two rolling backups, so a crash mid-write cannot eat your progress.
- The main menu shows which island you are on under the summary (**Slot: …**).
  **Load Save** loads it straight away when you have one island.
- Once you own **two or more islands**, **Load Save** opens the island picker:
  one row per island with its money, score, circuits, day count and how long ago you
  played, plus three actions — **Load island**, **✎ rename**, **✕ delete**.
  The list drag-scrolls on small screens or when the shelf fills up.
- **+ New island** at the top of the picker asks for a name and drops you
  straight onto a fresh shore, leaving your existing islands untouched. Duplicate
  names are fine — the folders get numbered.
- Renaming changes the label only; the island keeps its history, backups and
  achievements. Deleting asks for confirmation and wipes that island's save and
  both backups for good. Deleting the island you are on moves you to the most
  recent remaining one (or a fresh shore if none are left).
- Old single-file saves from earlier builds are migrated into a slot called
  **Island** the first time you launch, so nothing is lost on upgrade.

## Settings & touch
- The stats page's **Restore backup** button rolls the island back to its
  previous save (with a confirm). The current state is kept as the second
  backup.

- The island menu (☰) holds Master / Music / Ambience / SFX sliders and the
  **Reduce motion** toggle. Each slider sits on its own row with a big, fat
  touch track, so it is easy to grab on a phone without the labels moving.
- The in-run **Return to Map** buttons in both minigames are 48 px tall, and the
  shop and stats lists drag-scroll (mouse drag or finger) without stealing taps
  from the buttons inside them.
- Every shop button is ≥ 44 px tall — the row **Sell 1 / Sell 5 / Sell All**, the
  Buy buttons and the footer Return / Undo — so nothing needs a pinpoint tap; the
  two-column counter still fits a 1152×648 screen with no scrolling.
- The world-menu (☰) buttons match: the five upgrade buttons (Move / Range /
  Deep Cache / its tier-up / Inv Cap), the **Settings** toggle and the **Reduce
  motion** checkbox are each ≥ 44 px, so the whole island menu is thumb-friendly.

## Screen layout & toasts
- Everything obeys one 24 px screen margin. Centered **modal cards** (welcome
  guide, minigame briefings, the pause/Island Menu) sit on a full-rect
  `CenterContainer` inset by 24 px; non-modal **bottom cards** (tutorial step
  hints) sit in a bottom `Margin` column. Both register in a group
  (`ui_modal_card` / `hud_bottom_card`) so the toast queue and the tutorial
  director stack above / wait for them instead of overlapping.
- The pause/Island Menu card is 880 px-min with its own header row (title +
  live upgrade status), an ACTIONS column (Resume, Save & quit) beside the
  infobox, and a **Settings** view that swaps out the upgrades/progress pane
  so the card never outgrows a 1152×648 screen. Under **Reduce motion** the
  card simply appears; otherwise it fades in over 0.16 s.
- Minigame HUDs group their stat labels into a `UI/HUDStats` VBox pinned at
  the 24 px safe margin (20 px type).
- Toast notifications live in a bottom-centre lane, 24 px off the edge, 18 px
  type with a dark outline, and lift clear of any visible bottom-centre card.

## Upgrades
- Move speed +8% / level via trash.
- Collection radius +12px / level via trash.
- Deep Cache 30 score purchase and 15*tier upgrade increases reef debris; the
  world-menu button names the current/next per-run bonus (e.g. "+10 kg/run"), and
  the bonus is never wasted on a full bag (see the reef notes below). The score
  is a lifetime bank — both a dive (combo multiplier) and a bench run feed it,
  and the world HUD bar counts down to the next Deep Cache purchase.
- Inventory capacity +10 level via trash.

### Trash-upgrade payback audit (measured, pass 5)
*Trash costs are paid in **kg of held material** (`Inventory.spend` compares inventory
weight), and the bag is the hard cap on a loop's haul — so one loop banks at most your
bag capacity in material. Measured on the live dive payout: accepted Reef Debris equals
free capacity (10 kg at the starting bag, 20 kg at Bag Lv.1) for any haul ≥ capacity, and
each debris sorts 1:1 into a material.*
- **Inventory Capacity Lv.1 (6 kg):** ~0.6 loops. The cheapest first step on the terminal.
- **Move Speed Lv.1 (8 kg):** ~0.8 loops; **Lv.2 (12 kg):** ~1.2 loops. This is what the
  **Tune up the dock** nudge points at.
- **Collection Range Lv.1 (12 kg):** ~1.2 loops.
- Because bag income grows +10 kg/level while the costs grow only +3/+4/+6, every later
  tier costs *fewer* loops than the one before (Move Lv.3 ≈ 0.5 loops at a 30 kg bag).
  Note the cost is real, not trivial: paying 8–12 kg is most of a starting bag's haul,
  so a trash upgrade is a roughly one-loop commitment of material you could have sold.
  That is the intended first-upgrade shape — a modest, affordable step right after the
  tour, not a money sink — so **no prices changed.**
- **Tune-nudge timing (verified):** with the tour complete and an empty nudge history,
  the card does **not** appear at a 7 kg hold (below the 8 kg cheapest tier) and appears
  with the live cost ("8 trash") the moment the hold reaches 8 kg. It fires from the world
  HUD, never mid-sale, and persists `nudge_tune_shown` so it is once per save.

## World map & dock ambience
- Tap any walkable tile to walk there (BFS pathfinding); glowing pads are the
  portals to the bench, the reef dive, and the shop.
- Completing all three stations in a lap pays the **$15 island-circuit** bonus
  and the return card names your lap "Circuit No N".
- Once your almanac is filled, the far sailboat wears a warm gold trim —
  the sailors know the keeper walks the shore.
- The dock is alive even when you stand still: a **harbor cry** sounds every
  40–75 s (a lone, lower-pitched gull answering itself across the water), on top
  of the four gulls patrolling the open water east of the island. The harbor cry
  is audio-only, so it keeps playing even with **Reduced motion** on — where the
  birds hold still, the water still sounds like water. On Night Drift the
  sea answers with a low foghorn from somewhere out there, half the time —
  the one tide with a voice of its own. Slack Water takes the opposite
  deal: the answering gull never returns your call — nothing pushes, nothing
  hurries. Glass Calm sends both birds back twice as often, because the
  flock mobs the easy pickings the reef let go of overnight.
  Eel Run keys every gull up — hotter pitch, shorter patience.
- Under it all runs a continuous **surf wash**, a seamless 12 s loop that fades
  in when you reach the map and keeps rolling while the pause menu is open. It
  has its own **Ambience** slider (settings: Master / Music / Ambience / SFX), so
  you can quiet the wash and wildlife without softening the sort/sale feedback.
- Bottles wash up on the shore with lore; after the Tidekeeper's Pact is funded
  and all base bottles are read, a hidden fourth bottle surfaces.
- The island has one more character nobody cast. **Corl**, the dockhand, works
  the shop counter with Kai and sells exactly one thing: tomorrow's tide, $25,
  cash, one day. Nobody asked him to — the sea started telling him, and Corl
  being Corl, he decided to share it at a price. He is never seen on the map;
  his only lines live in the shop's transaction log and the receipt. Oda, who
  watches the water for a living, finds this both hilarious and irritating —
  and the counter remembers every one of your visits; buy enough days and
  Corl will finally speak to you like a person — and a seventh whisper is
  rewarded with a Folded Paper on the shop path; the stats page ledger keeps
  a re-readable copy (six bottles plus the watcher's blessing). After THE KEEPER'S STAR is found, an eighth and final note — the far point's own reply, the epilogue's paper tail — washes up at the tip of the island; the Tide Ledger keeps it with the rest.
  He marks the calendar too — day 10, 30 and 50 each earn exactly one
  line from the counter, and he never says any of them twice.

## The island pays attention (NPC milestones)
Every named islander notices what you do once you do it. The game tracks no
new flags for this — it reads the achievement catalogue, and once a badge is
earned the relevant NPC appends a line to every later conversation:

- **Mara** — TIDEKEEPER's Pact funded (belief), LOOP MASTER (25 circuits),
  CLEAN SWEEP (five perfect dives), THE FAR POINT (she started the rumour;
  she raised you right).
- **Tom** — the three shore notes read (TIDE KEEPER), HEAVY HARVEST (30 kg in
  one dive), REEF VETERAN (25 dives), TO THE ABYSS (900 m), THE FAR POINT
  ("I keep to my fishing").
- **Nina** — the three shore notes read, SILK TOUCH (flawless shifts), THE
  FAR POINT (word travels at the counter).
- **Kai** — TIDEKEEPER's Pact funded, GOLDEN TOUCH (ten golden scraps),
  THE WHISPERER (Corl's seven days), THE SILENT CHORUS (six bottles; the seventh note is a blessing, not a requirement), THE
  FAR POINT (he will not restock it).
- **Oda** — THE KEEPER'S LETTER, THE SILENT CHORUS, THE LONG KEEPER (ten
  days on the almanac), ISLAND STEWARD, THE STEADY KEEPER (three running),
  THE FAR POINT ("Some of us watch the water. Some of us are watched by it.").

Milestones stack: an NPC can notice several things at once. Completing the
whole catalogue (ISLAND STEWARD) earns a personal send-off from every one of
them — and a faint golden hush over the world.

## The sound of the tide
- **Glass Calm** — the harbor cries back twice as often; the flock mobs the easy pickings.
- **Eel Run** — every gull in the sky runs hot (0.95–1.08 pitch). The hunt is on.
- **Slack Water** — the answering gull never returns your call. Nothing pushes, nothing hurries.
- **Night Drift** — half the distant cries become a low foghorn, far out on the water.
